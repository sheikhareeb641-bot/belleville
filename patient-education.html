Patient Education

Simple, trustworthy information about your teeth, gums and mouth — learn how daily habits and regular check-ups prevent decay, gum disease and costly treatment later.

Oral Health

Good oral health is more than a pretty smile

Oral (dental) health concerns the teeth, the gums, the tongue and the entire mouth. A healthy mouth lets you eat and speak comfortably, protects you from pain and infection, and supports your overall health.

Most oral problems are preventable. Brushing, flossing, a balanced diet and regular dental visits keep plaque under control — and plaque is the main cause of both tooth decay and gum disease.

  • Brush for two minutes, twice a day
  • Floss daily to remove plaque between teeth
  • Drink water and limit sugary snacks
  • See your dentist every six months

Dental Awareness

Essential topics for every patient

Understand the most common dental problems and the simple habits that prevent them.

Tooth Decay

Decay happens when bacteria in plaque turn sugar into acids that dissolve tooth enamel, creating cavities. The first sign is often sensitivity or a hole you can feel. Early treatment is simple; late treatment may need a root canal.

Prevention tip Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, spit — don’t rinse — and cut down on sugary drinks between meals.
Gum Disease

Gingivitis (red, bleeding gums) is the first stage and is fully reversible. Untreated, it becomes periodontitis: gums pull away from the teeth, bone is lost and teeth may become loose. Bleeding while brushing is never normal.

Prevention tip Floss daily to clean where brushes cannot reach, and book a professional cleaning every six months.
Brushing

Use a soft-bristled brush and a pea-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste. Hold the brush at a 45° angle to the gum line and use gentle circular motions — brushing hard damages gums and enamel. Replace your brush every three months.

Prevention tip Two minutes, twice a day — use a timer or an electric brush with a built-in timer so no area is missed.
Flossing

A brush cleans only about 60% of the tooth surface. Floss removes plaque and food from between teeth and under the gum line — the places where cavities often start. Guide the floss in a C-shape around each tooth and slide it under the gum.

Prevention tip Floss once a day, ideally before brushing, so fluoride from the toothpaste reaches freshly cleaned gaps.
Preventive Dental Care

Prevention costs a fraction of treatment. Fluoride varnish, dental sealants, mouthguards, a balanced diet and avoiding tobacco all dramatically reduce the risk of decay, gum disease and oral cancer.

Prevention tip Ask your dentist about sealants for back teeth and fluoride varnish if you are cavity-prone.
Regular Dental Visits

A check-up catches problems while they are small and painless. Your dentist also screens for gum disease, oral cancer and bite problems, and cleans hardened plaque (tartar) that brushing cannot remove.

Prevention tip Visit every six months — or sooner if you notice bleeding gums, bad breath, pain or sensitivity.

Daily Routine

The right way to brush & floss

Brushing routine

Morning: brush for two minutes after breakfast. Night: brush for two minutes before bed — the most important brush of the day, because saliva flow drops while you sleep.

Flossing routine

Use about 45 cm of floss, wrap it around your middle fingers, and guide it gently between each tooth. Never snap the floss into the gums. Floss picks are a great alternative for children and beginners.
